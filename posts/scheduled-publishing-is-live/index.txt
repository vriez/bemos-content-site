11:T42b,<h1>This post scheduled itself</h1>
<p>If you're reading this on the live site, the scheduling pipeline works. This</p>
<p>post was authored and committed <strong>before</strong> its publish time, marked</p>
<p><code>status: published</code> with a <strong>future</strong> <code>publishDate</code>. It stayed invisible until</p>
<p>that moment passed — then the next automated rebuild put it live.</p>
<h2>What happened behind the scenes</h2>
<ul>
<li>The post sat in the repo, <code>published</code> but <strong>scheduled</strong> for a future time.</li>
<li>Every build compares <code>publishDate</code> against the current time (see <code>isLive</code>),</li>
</ul>
<p>so the post rendered to <em>nobody</em> until its moment arrived.</p>
<ul>
<li>A timed CI job rebuilds and redeploys production on a schedule. The first</li>
</ul>
<p>rebuild after the publish time swept this post live — **no human, no push,</p>
<p>no code deploy.**</p>
<p>That's the whole promise of BEMA-4: author once, schedule, and let the</p>
